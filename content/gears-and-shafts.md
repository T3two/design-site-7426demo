# Gears & Shafts


Gears are categorized as the transmission of rotary power on a toothed wheel onto another toothed part.

## The Spur gear




Two toothed wheels with straight cut teeth cut parallel with the axis of rotation, they are usually defined by diametral pitch (also known as DP). Which just means how many teeth per inch are in its pitch diameter (Pitch diameter is the diameter of the pitch circle, where two mating gears are considered to be tangent and where their rotational motion is effectively transmitted). Note that if one gear goes clockwise, the other one will go the opposite direction.

EXAMPLE: Team 48 – E.L.I.T.E. used spur-gear reductions in their 2023 robot's arm. Their J3 joint included a 60:12 spur reduction, followed by additional reductions

Why does the motor shaft look so funny compared to the ½ hex we discussed?

Splined shafts offer several advantages over the traditional round and keyed shafts commonly used on FRC motors. Instead of transferring torque through a single key, splines use multiple teeth to connect the motor shaft to a gear, pulley, or other component. This distributes the load across a larger area, allowing the connection to handle higher torque while reducing stress concentrations that can occur around keyways. Splines can also reduce backlash, helping mechanisms maintain more accurate positioning, especially in applications like swerve modules and arms.

They also simplify the mechanical design and assembly process. A keyed system requires a shaft, key, keyway, and matching component, meaning there are more individual parts that can be lost, damaged, or improperly assembled. Splined interfaces eliminate the need for a separate key by directly engaging the shaft and component. This can make assemblies more reliable and easier to service while also improving fatigue life by distributing forces more uniformly across the connection.

Remember how I mentioned in the NEO that it uses a press fit? Spline solves the need for such an issue since a matching splined gear can simply slide onto the shaft and be retained in place. Overall, splines provide a stronger, more precise, and more convenient connection between a motor and the rest of the mechanism which then in turn, makes them especially useful in high-performance FRC designs.
